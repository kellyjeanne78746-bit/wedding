Getting There
Travelling to Italy

Rome is well-connected by two airports. We recommend flying into Fiumicino (FCO) for the best connections. Book accommodation early — late May is beautiful in Rome!

✈️ Flights

  • From the USA — Fly into Fiumicino Airport (FCO) for international arrivals
  • From within the EU — Either Ciampino (CIA) or Fiumicino (FCO) work well

🚆 Trains & Getting Around

  • Want to see Rome before or after the wedding? Take the train from FCO airport directly to Termini Station in central Rome
  • To reach your hotel for the wedding — From Termini, take the “Castelli Romani” regional line to Frascati
  • Uber works in Rome — and we also recommend the FREE NOW taxi app

📋 Things to Remember

  • ETIAS is required for visa-exempt travelers entering the Schengen Area — apply online before you fly
  • Check your passport — make sure it's valid for at least 6 months beyond your travel dates
  • Currency — Italy uses the Euro (€). Cards are widely accepted; carry some cash for cafés and small shops

While You're Here
Explore Italy

For those making a trip out of the celebration, here are a few of our favorite places and experiences in Rome. Whether it's your first visit or a return, there's no better place to wander, dine, and take it all in.

Eat & Drink

A few of our favorites while in Rome. We recommend making reservations when possible, especially for dinner.

Breakfast
Cornetto & Cappuccino
The Roman morning ritual — a flaky cornetto and a cappuccino at the bar of a neighborhood pasticceria. Try the little bakeries around Piazza Navona and Campo de' Fiori — the pastries are extraordinary.
Pizza
Pizzeria Da Baffetto
For the best pizza — Roman-style, thin, crisp, and unmistakable. A no-frills institution near Piazza Navona, just a few blocks from the Pantheon. Expect a queue; expect it to be worth it.
Pasta
Da Dino e Tony
Our pick for the best pasta in Rome — tucked near the Vatican.
Aperitivo
A Spritz Before Dinner
The Roman ritual — a spritz or glass of wine at a local bar before dinner. Find a piazza, find a seat, watch the city go by.
Gelato
Venchi
For the best gelato — silky, rich, and made with their own chocolate. Multiple locations across the city.
Dessert
I Tre Scalini
For the best Tartufo — the legendary chocolate dessert in Piazza Navona. A Roman institution.

Tours & Excursions

From Rome's most iconic landmarks to favorite day trips beyond the city — toggle between the two below.

Rome's most iconic landmarks — best visited early in the morning or later in the evening.

Historic Site
The Colosseum
The heart of ancient Rome. Book skip-the-line tickets in advance — and consider the after-hours night tour for an unforgettable, crowd-free experience.
Book tickets
Landmark
Trevi Fountain
Toss a coin over your shoulder to ensure your return to Rome — early morning or late evening is best to avoid the crowds.
Landmark
Spanish Steps
A grand staircase of 135 steps connecting Piazza di Spagna to the Trinità dei Monti church above.
Ancient Wonder
The Pantheon
Nearly 2,000 years old and still the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome. The oculus is breathtaking.
Landmark
Vatican City
The Vatican Museums, Sistine Chapel, and St. Peter's Basilica. Book early morning entry to avoid the crowds.
Reserve entry
Neighborhood
Trastevere
Cobblestone streets, ivy-covered buildings, and the best trattorias in Rome. Perfect for an evening passeggiata.
Garden
Villa Borghese Gardens
A peaceful escape in the heart of Rome — winding paths, fountains, and the Galleria Borghese inside.
View
Il Pincio
The terrace at the edge of Villa Borghese — one of the most romantic sunset views in Rome, looking out over Piazza del Popolo and the city's domes.

For guests extending the celebration with side trips — a lakeside afternoon next door, a coastal day, or a longer escape into the hills.

~20 min from venue · ~30–40 min from Rome · Half-day
Castel Gandolfo & Lake Albano
The Pope's summer residence above a volcanic crater lake. Wander the cobblestone piazza, lunch lakeside, swim or rent a paddleboat. The closest excursion to the venue.
~30 min from venue · ~15–20 min from Rome · Half-day
Parco degli Acquedotti
Walk or bike beneath kilometers of intact Roman aqueducts striding across an open meadow on the edge of Rome — one of the most cinematic and surprisingly under-visited spots in the area.
~45 min from venue · ~30–45 min from Rome · Full day
Tivoli — Villa d'Este & Hadrian's Villa
Two UNESCO World Heritage sites in one hill town: the Renaissance fountain gardens of Villa d'Este, and the sprawling 2nd-century ruins of Emperor Hadrian's country estate.
~1.5 hrs from venue · ~1.5 hrs from Rome · Day trip
Sperlonga — Tyrrhenian Coast
A whitewashed cliffside village over a sweep of clear sea. Lunch on grilled fish, swim from the cove beach, and visit the Tiberius grottoes. Drive or take the train to Fondi-Sperlonga + bus.
~30 min from venue · ~20–30 min from Rome · Half or full day
Tenuta Principe Alberico
Organic Antinori-family wine estate on the historic Appian Way. Wine tastings, picnics among the vines, and farm-to-table lunch at L'Orto di Alberico — chef Daniele Rossi cooks from the estate's own gardens.
~1 hr from venue · ~1 hr from Rome · Half-day
Olive Oil Tour — Sabine Hills
Cypress-lined lanes through ancient olive groves in the Sabina, just northeast of Rome. Visit a family-run frantoio (olive mill), taste freshly pressed extra-virgin oils paired with local breads and wines.
~4 hrs from Rome by train · Overnight
Cinque Terre
Five jewel-box villages tumbling down the Ligurian cliffs into the sea. Hike between them, swim from the rocky coves, and feast on seafood and pesto. Best as an overnight from Rome — train to La Spezia, then the Cinque Terre regional line.
~2 hrs from venue · ~2 hrs from Rome · Day trip
Civita di Bagnoregio
"La città che muore" — a tiny medieval village perched on a tufa pinnacle, reached only by a slender footbridge. Surreal at sunrise or sunset, easy to combine with a stop in Orvieto.

A Few Notes

Small tips that go a long way during your stay.

What to Wear
Stylish yet relaxed
Comfortable, breathable clothing and good walking shoes are key. If you plan to visit churches, bring a light layer or scarf — many feel like museums but remain active places of worship.
Getting Around
On foot is best
Walking is often the easiest way to experience the city. Taxis are readily available when needed, and Uber/FREE NOW work well.
Carrying Money
Cards plus a little cash
Credit cards are widely accepted, but it's helpful to carry some euros for smaller cafés, taxis, or shops.
Tours & Tickets
Book in advance
For major sites like the Colosseum and Vatican Museums, reserve tickets ahead of time to avoid long lines.
